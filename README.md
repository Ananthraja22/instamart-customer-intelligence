# Overview

The Instamart Customer Intelligence & Support Copilot is one internal tool built in four connected Parts. Part A turns messy order exports into a trusted SQLite database. Part B uses that database to segment customers and predict who will lapse. Part C triages support tickets safely and routes them using the Part B segments. Part D answers policy questions and runs a refund agent against the Part A database, reusing one policy retrieval path.

All data is synthetic and fictional, produced by `generate_data.py`. Every language-model call goes through the supplied offline `mock_llm.py`, so no key, account or network is needed. All money is in INR.

| Folder or file | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `generate_data.py`, `mock_llm.py` | Supplied files, unmodified |
| `scripts/a*.py` | Part A: audit, clean and store, validate, SQL, exploration |
| `scripts/b*.py` | Part B: segments, lapse model |
| `scripts/c1_triage.py` | Part C: triage pipeline |
| `scripts/d*.py` | Part D: policy assistant, refund agent |
| `sql/queries.sql` | The seven analytical queries |

`data/`, `outputs/` and `state/` are regenerated by the commands below and are not committed.

# Reproduce

Run from the repository root with Python 3.11 or newer. The scripts take about a minute, not counting the install. On Windows, step 2 is `.venv\Scripts\activate`.

1. `python -m venv .venv`
2. `source .venv/bin/activate`
3. `python -m pip install -r requirements.txt`
4. `python generate_data.py`
5. `python scripts/a1_audit.py`
6. `python scripts/a2_clean_store.py`
7. `python scripts/a3_validate.py`
8. `python scripts/a4_sql.py`
9. `python scripts/a5_eda.py`
10. `python scripts/b1_segments.py`
11. `python scripts/b2_lapse.py`
12. `python scripts/c1_triage.py`
13. `python scripts/d1_policy_rag.py`
14. `python scripts/d2_refund_agent.py`

Step 4 ends with `generated: customers=1200 products=40 orders_raw=13084 tickets=164`. Step 7 prints `ALL CHECKPOINTS PASSED`. Scripts assert the brief's checkpoints as they run, so a mismatch stops with an error.

**What is this data about?** Six months of synthetic quick-commerce orders, April to September 2025, from 1,200 customers in six Indian metros buying 40 products across five categories.

**What do its distributions and statistics look like?** Completed order value is strongly right-skewed: the mean of INR 269.91 is about double the median of INR 135.00, with skewness 4.61. Most baskets are small staples and a minority of expensive orders pull the mean up.

## Choosing K

| K | Inertia | Silhouette | Inertia drop from previous K |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 1820.8 | 0.465 | |
| 3 | 1268.2 | 0.445 | 30.3% |
| 4 | 743.1 | 0.526 | 41.4% |
| 5 | 617.4 | 0.480 | 16.9% |
| 6 | 503.2 | 0.482 | 18.5% |
| 7 | 408.5 | 0.416 | 18.8% |
| 8 | 366.5 | 0.412 | 10.3% |

**Chosen K = 4.** The elbow is at K=4: inertia falls 41.4% reaching it and no more than 18.8% at any later step. K=4 also has the best silhouette (0.526), and its four groups each call for a different campaign.

The inertia of K=8 is always lower than that of K=2 because extra centroids can only shorten each customer's distance to the nearest one, reaching zero when every customer has their own cluster. A measure that always improves as K grows would always pick the largest K, so it cannot choose K alone. That is why the elbow is read together with silhouette and with whether the business can act on the groups.

## Lapse problem

The cutoff is 2025-09-01. The cohort is 1,156 customers, of whom 443 lapsed (38.32%). The split gives 924 training and 232 test rows. Features are recency, frequency, monetary, average discount, cancellation rate, average delivery minutes, age, tenure in days, city, gender and favourite category.

**Leakage statement.** No encoder, scaler or resampler saw test rows: they are all fitted inside a Pipeline on training rows only. No data from 2025-09-01 onwards entered the features, and the segment labels from Part B's clustering were not used as features. Part A filled some delivery times from whole-window medians that include September, so Part B replaces those values with pre-cutoff medians of measured times. This matters only slightly: the Personal Care median is 25.5 minutes across the whole window and 25.0 before the cutoff, which changes 39 customers' feature.

The learned scaler mean of `frequency` is 8.1147 against a full-cohort mean of 8.0285; they differ because the scaler saw only the 924 training rows, not the 232 test rows. A code check confirms no feature column contains a missing value.

## Tuning and evaluation

| Model | Best parameters | Best CV F1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logistic regression | C=100 | 0.651 |
| Random forest (45 fits) | max_depth=8, min_samples_leaf=10 | 0.682 |

Logistic regression's CV F1 is nearly flat above C=0.1 (0.646, 0.645, 0.649 and 0.651 for C=0.1, 1, 10 and 100), and C=1, 10 and 100 give the same test F1, so the best C here being 100 is not a meaningful difference.

Lapsed-class results on the untouched test set:

| Model | TN | FP | FN | TP | Accuracy | Precision | Recall | F1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline (always not lapsed) | 143 | 0 | 89 | 0 | 61.6% | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| Logistic regression | 111 | 32 | 31 | 58 | 72.8% | 0.644 | 0.652 | 0.648 |
| Random forest | 113 | 30 | 31 | 58 | 73.7% | 0.659 | 0.652 | 0.655 |

**Selected model: random forest.** Missing a lapsing customer loses all their future orders, while wrongly targeting a loyal one costs only a coupon, so misses are the more expensive error. Both models miss the same 31 of 89 lapsers, so they tie on that cost. The forest wrongly targets 30 loyal customers against 32 and also has the higher cross-validated F1 (0.682 against 0.651). The test gap is only two customers, so this is a narrow preference rather than a decisive win.

## Class-balance experiment

Over-sampling added 216 duplicate lapsed rows to the training set only, giving 570 of each class. Logistic regression (C=100) was refit and scored on the unchanged test set.

| Metric | Unbalanced | Balanced | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Precision | 0.644 | 0.589 | −0.055 |
| Recall | 0.652 | 0.854 | +0.202 |
| F1 | 0.648 | 0.697 | +0.049 |

Recall moved most. For the retention team, the balanced model catches 76 of 89 lapsers instead of 58, at the price of 53 loyal customers wrongly targeted instead of 32: 18 more saves to attempt for 21 more unnecessary coupons.

## Prompts, retries and validation

Both system prompts contain `CLASSIFY`, the four categories and the required JSON reply form. The zero-shot prompt has no examples. The few-shot prompt adds a line `EXAMPLES:` followed by four labelled examples, one of them fraud.

Each call allows up to 3 retries with a wait of `0.05 × 2**n × random.uniform(0.9, 1.15)` seconds, logging one line per retry, for example `[retry] ticket=T0002 retry=0 wait=0.053s`. Each full run produced exactly 19 `TransientLLMError`s, all recovered by a retry, and no ticket ended with a permanent error.

# Limitations and Next Steps

- **Premium flag:** the Premium flag is a post-hoc whole-window label that includes the September window defining the lapse label, so the retention list is illustrative and not a leakage-free evaluation. Next step: recompute segments at the cutoff before ranking customers.
- **Shared imputation:** Part A's city-median ages and Part B's pre-cutoff delivery medians are computed across all customers, including test rows. The brief mandates the age rule and the effect is very small, but a production model should learn these from training rows only.
- **Model choice:** the test set was used to compare the two tuned models, so its F1 is slightly optimistic. The balanced logistic regression scored higher (F1 0.697) and catches more lapsers, so it should be validated on fresh data as a recall-first alternative.
- **Retention ranking:** the model is driven mainly by frequency and did not rank lapsers well within the Premium segment. Next step: train or calibrate specifically for high-value customers.
- **Mock model:** classifier accuracy and agent behaviour reflect `mock_llm.py`'s fixed rules. A real model needs its own evaluation, and the budget and retry settings would need retuning.
- **Refund rules:** the 7-day request window cannot be checked because the data has no request date. A completed order with an imputed delivery time is treated as measured, so it is always denied as on time; such orders should go to a human.
- **Escalations:** `escalate_to_human` records the handoff only in the run log, so a real system needs a persistent queue with de-duplication.
- **Fraud in human review:** a fraud ticket whose model reply fails validation goes to human review on the normal deadline rather than the 2-hour one, because the pipeline never guesses a category. Next step: add a keyword check that raises its priority without classifying it.
